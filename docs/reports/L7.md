# L7: License feasibility scan (MotionBricks pipeline)

Scanned 2026-11-07. Engineering research, not legal advice. Tags: **[V]** = VERIFIED (seen in text; source given). **[I]** = INFERRED (my reading or a gap, not stated in the text).

## 1. Verdict: YELLOW (core path) / RED (some components, if their output ships)

- **Core path, YELLOW.** MotionBricks code (Apache-2.0), weights (NVIDIA Open Model Agreement), SOMA-X (Apache-2.0) and GEM-X (Apache-2.0 code, NVIDIA Open Model License weights) all say commercial use is permitted [V]. Obligations are notices and attribution. Open items: the exact agreement version for GEM-X, the Unity runtime terms, and the license for the doji Unity package.
- **RED if shipped:** (a) the MotionBricks **sample motions** (evaluation only, internal, no distribution) [V]; (b) **SA.ReactionControl** (GPL-3.0, upstream says it is a reverse-engineered port of Euphoria) [V]; (c) any motion produced through **RGBMoCap's noncommercial models** (GVHMR, ExMR, PromptHMR, MAMMA) or **SMPL-family / MANO body files**, because those licenses bar producing artifacts for commercial purposes [V]. Whether captured motion counts as such an "artifact" is a lawyer question, but the text points against commercial use.
- **Private use now:** generally fine, with two exceptions. The sample motions may be used only to evaluate NVIDIA technology [V]. The MPI licenses allow only non-commercial research, education or art projects [V]. Whether a private project built to make a game for sale counts as "non-commercial" is a question for a lawyer [I].

### 2a. Licensed reference video: what to check (no conclusion)

1. Does the grant cover **derivative works** or **data extraction**, or only "use in a production" as footage? Is pose estimation or motion capture named? [I]
2. Do any **AI/ML clauses** ban using the footage as input to AI systems, or for training? [I]
3. **Model and talent releases**: do they cover the performer's movement or likeness in a different medium (a game character)? Distinctive performances such as dance choreography or signature moves may carry separate rights [I].
4. **Media and distribution scope**: interactive or video-game use, territory, term, seat limits, and whether the license survives expiry for already-shipped products [I].
5. **Editorial-only** labels, and any bans on "sensitive use" or on digital replicas or biometric processing [I].
6. GEM-X's card asks users to hold proper rights to input video [V: huggingface.co/nvidia/GEM-X].

## 3. Conflicts

- **GPL-3.0 (SA.ReactionControl) versus a closed sold game, Rukhanka's EULA, and the Unity runtime.** Distributing a combined work requires GPL terms for the whole work. The Asset Store EULA forbids redistributing Rukhanka source, so a GPL release including Rukhanka looks impossible [I]. provenance.md already says ReactionControl must stay private [V].
- **Noncommercial capture models versus commercial game motion.** MPI licenses (SMPL-X, MANO, MAMMA, PromptHMR) bar commercial artifacts. GVHMR and ExMR are limited to research and non-profit use [V]. That conflicts with shipping RGBMoCap-captured motion. GEM-X (commercial-ready, SOMA geometry) is the documented commercial-ready alternative [V].
- **Two NVIDIA agreements.** MotionBricks uses the Open Model *Agreement* (no guardrail or Trustworthy AI clause found). GEM-X and possibly motion-bricks.cpp's GGUF use the Open Model *License* Agreement (guardrail, Trustworthy AI, NVIDIA may update the terms) [V]. Track which governs each file.
- **AGPL/GPL tool dependencies** (YOLO, DroidCalib, HTD-Refine, FFmpeg builds) matter only if the tooling is distributed. The tools are not shipped in the game [I].
- **Sample motions versus MotionBricks weights.** Same repo, different licenses. Clips authored from the samples cannot ship [V].

## 5. Risks and answers to specific questions

**Who owns generated motion?** The Open Model Agreement preamble says NVIDIA claims no ownership of outputs from the Works or Derivative Works [V: nvidia.com/en-us/agreements/enterprise-software/nvidia-open-model-agreement/]. The Open Model License (GEM-X) §2.4 says the same and makes users responsible for outputs [V: nvidia.com/en-us/agreements/enterprise-software/nvidia-open-model-license/]. Neither agreement assigns ownership to you or says outputs are copyrightable. Whether AI-generated motion can be protected at all is a lawyer question [I]. The training data (Bones RP1) owner's position on outputs is unknown [I].

**Terms limiting games or competing-model use?** In the Open Model Agreement, the fetch found no field-of-use, game, guardrail or competing-model restriction [V: OMA URL]. The Open Model License (GEM-X) has no game or competitor clause, but it has guardrail and Trustworthy AI conditions and a special-purpose-model limitation (§1.5, §6) [V]. GEM-X's card lists gaming as an intended use [V]. The Sample Data license allows only internal evaluation of NVIDIA technologies, which rules out game use [V]. The MPI licenses bar training models for commercial use [V].

**What ends the license?**
- *Open Model Agreement*, §2: licenses for a Work end on the filing date if you sue alleging the Work or its outputs infringe a patent or copyright. No other termination trigger was found. The grant is otherwise described as irrevocable [V].
- *Open Model License*, §2.1: the same litigation trigger, plus automatic termination for bypassing guardrails, and NVIDIA may update the terms [V].
- *Sample Data license*, §6: ends automatically on any breach or on a lawsuit against NVIDIA about the data. NVIDIA may also terminate at any time with written notice. You must then destroy the data and derivatives [V].
- *MPI licenses*: end automatically on any infringement [V].
- *Asset Store EULA*: the licensor may terminate on breach [V §4.1].
- *Apache-2.0*: the patent license ends if you bring patent litigation over the Work [I: standard Apache §3, not re-read].

**NVIDIA attribution requirements.** Open Model Agreement §3: give recipients a copy of the agreement, keep notices, and include a Notice text file reading "Licensed by NVIDIA Corporation under the NVIDIA Open Model Agreement." [V: OMA URL; also `motionbricks/assets/onnx/LICENSE.md`]. Open Model License §3.1: Notice file with "...under the NVIDIA Open Model License" plus a copy of the agreement [V]. Neither allows NVIDIA trademark use beyond describing origin [V]. The MotionBricks model card asks for a citation (project page and arXiv 2604.24833) [V: julienkay HF card].

**Key risks to take to a lawyer**
1. SA.ReactionControl: GPL plus possible NaturalMotion/Rockstar rights in reverse-engineered logic.
2. Using RGBMoCap output (SMPL-X fitted; GVHMR, ExMR, PromptHMR, MAMMA) in a sold game.
3. Licensed reference video: whether it covers motion extraction (checklist in 2a).
4. Indemnity: both NVIDIA model agreements make you indemnify NVIDIA for third-party claims tied to your use or outputs [V §7 / OMA].
5. Bones RP1 training-data provenance, and any downstream limits on generated motion.
6. Unknown license for tools.doji.motionbricks.
